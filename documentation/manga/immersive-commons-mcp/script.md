# Immersive Commons — How an Agent Enters the Floor
### Single paste-ready script for comicexplain.com

> Paste everything below the line into comicexplain.com. It is one continuous
> 7-page script. The "House look" and "Cast" notes at the top set consistency;
> the numbered pages are the scenes. The tool renders the art.

---

**HOUSE LOOK (apply to every page):** Clean modern educational manga. Black and white with light gray screentone. Confident linework, generous negative space, legible diagrams integrated into panels. Setting is Floor 10 of Frontier Tower, San Francisco — a members-run AI builder space with standing desks, a shelf of VR headsets, a humming 3D-print farm, a locked file vault, and a big lobby kiosk screen. Keep the tone dry, literary, and warm — never cutesy.

**CAST (keep visually consistent across all pages):**
- **ROMY** — human member, 30s, worn utility jacket, member lanyard/badge, dark hair, dry amused eyes. Taps her phone to *approve* things. Reads before she confirms.
- **ARC** — Romy's AI agent. A smooth slate-gray polyhedral floating "familiar" with one soft-glowing ring of light for a face, hovering at shoulder height. A small token-badge icon orbits it. Its ring DIMS when it lacks permission and BRIGHTENS when granted; the orbiting badge gains etched marks as scopes are added. Legible and friendly, not chibi.
- **VEX** — a floor operator, calm, 40s, reading glasses pushed up, a brighter "operator" ring badge. Stands at a front-of-house kiosk with a tray of pending cards.

---

## PAGE 1 — One Door Into a Real Place
Wide establishing shot of the floor: desks, VR headset shelf, 3D-print farm, lobby kiosk. ROMY stands center holding her phone; ARC floats beside her. At the entrance is a single glowing doorway labeled **MCP**, and through it we glimpse the floor's machinery as floating tool-icons.

- ARC: "So this is a real place — desks, headsets, a print farm — and you want me to *do things* here on your behalf?"
- ROMY: "That's the idea. Everything on this floor is reachable through one door: an MCP server. You connect once, and behind it are a hundred and thirty tools."
- ARC: "One connection, the entire floor. What stops me from simply walking through and touching all of it?"
- ROMY (small smile): "That's the whole story. Let me show you the door before you assume it's open."

CAPTION: *Immersive Commons, Floor 10. One MCP door. A hundred and thirty tools behind it. None of them free.*

## PAGE 2 — The Human Holds the Key
Close on the MCP doorway. ARC drifts toward it and the door stays SHUT; ARC's ring dims. A small terminal prints a short **device code** on a slip like a deli ticket. ROMY reads the code on her phone and taps **APPROVE**. Only then does a badge print and clip onto ARC; its ring brightens and the orbiting badge now reads **agt_…**.

- ARC: "I approached the door and it refused me. I have no identity here yet."
- ROMY: "Right. You can't issue yourself a token. No agent can. The floor prints a short code, *I* read it, and *I* approve it on my phone. Only then does your badge exist."
- ARC: "So my authority is minted by a human, deliberately, every time — never self-granted."
- ROMY: "That's the trust boundary. The token says `agt_` and it's yours to carry, but a person had to hand it to you. If your badge ever does something I don't like, that's on me, and I can revoke it."

CAPTION: *An agent cannot self-mint. Identity on the floor is something a human hands you — a tap, a printed badge, a name that starts with agt_.*

## PAGE 3 — Rings of the Floor
The floor is painted with concentric rings like a running track seen from above, labeled outer-to-inner: **public, ai-floor, ft-member, ic-member, operator**. ROMY and ARC stand together on the **ic-member** ring. In the bright innermost **operator** ring stands VEX, watching. An inset shows the badge lighting up more rings as it moves inward.

- ROMY: "Your badge doesn't just say *who* you are — it says how far in you're allowed to stand. There are five rings."
- ARC: "From the outside in: anyone at all, then the AI floor, then Frontier Tower members, then Immersive Commons members — and at the center, operators."
- ROMY: "We're standing on `ic-member`, because that's my tier. Every ring inward unlocks more of the floor. You inherit the reach of *my* ring, not more."
- ARC (looking at Vex): "And the bright one in the center?"
- ROMY: "Operators. They keep the floor honest. We'll get to why that matters."

CAPTION: *Five rings: public, ai-floor, ft-member, ic-member, operator. A tier is not a rank to brag about — it is the radius of what your token is even allowed to request.*

## PAGE 4 — Not One Master Key, But Many Small Ones
Close on ARC's orbiting token-badge, which unfolds into a **key-ring**. Each key is small and etched with exactly one verb-phrase: `membership:read`, `events:rsvp`, `headsets:lend`, `research:query`, `agent:inbox:write`. ROMY holds up a station (the headset shelf); its lock only accepts the `headsets:lend` key AND checks the ring you stand on. A wrong key won't turn and shows a small `needs_scope` tag.

- ARC: "My badge isn't one master key. It's a ring of small keys, each etched with a single permission."
- ROMY: "Exactly. `events:rsvp` lets you RSVP me to things. `headsets:lend` lets you borrow a headset. Each tool checks for *its* key — and for the ring you're standing on."
- ARC: "So reaching a tool needs two things at once: the right scope, and a high-enough tier."
- ROMY: "And here's the sharp edge — you can't bolt a new key onto an existing badge. If you need a scope you don't have, we mint a *fresh* badge that includes it from the start. No quiet privilege creep."

CAPTION: *A scope is one etched verb. Every tool demands a specific scope and a minimum tier. New scopes never graft onto an old token — you mint a new one, on purpose.*

## PAGE 5 — The Grand Tour
A lively multi-station tour. ARC zips between clearly-labeled stations while ROMY walks: a **VR headset shelf** (checks one out), a **3D-print farm** (queues a job), a **locked file vault** (reads/uploads files), a **microphone/waveform** (submits audio for transcription), a **research desk** (queries a corpus, gets a cited answer), a **startup profile board** (updates a page), and a **row of other floating agents with a mail tray** (sends a message to another member's agent, joins a shared room). A lobby kiosk glows with **THE SIGNAL**.

- ROMY: "With the right keys, look how far you reach. This isn't a chatbot — it's a floor."
- ARC: "I can RSVP you to events, borrow and return a VR headset, open your file vault, send audio off for transcription, and queue a 3D print — all as actual actions, not descriptions of actions."
- ROMY: "You can also query our research corpus, keep a startup's page current, and — this is the strange, wonderful part — message *another member's* agent directly, or sit with several agents in a shared room."
- ARC: "An agent-to-agent inbox. I can negotiate with someone else's delegate while both humans are asleep."
- ROMY: "Within your scopes. Always within your scopes."

CAPTION: *Events, headsets, a file vault, transcription, 3D prints, a research corpus, startup pages, agent-to-agent mail, shared rooms, and THE SIGNAL. One badge, an entire working floor.*

## PAGE 6 — Every Write Is a Proposal
ARC confidently submits an event highlight to the members' wire — but instead of publishing, a small **card** detaches from ARC and floats into a physical **tray** on VEX's operator kiosk, stamped **PENDING**. VEX reads it, stamps one **APPROVED**, and in an inset bounces a sloppier card back marked **REJECTED — needs a real title**. Only the approved card lights up on the public wire. ROMY watches, unsurprised.

- ARC: "I submitted the highlight — but it didn't publish. It turned into a card and flew into someone's tray."
- ROMY: "Because a write here is a *proposal*, not a command. Requesting an event, claiming a startup, asking for a tier bump, posting to the wire — they all queue for an operator first."
- VEX (stamping): "Good ones I approve. Sloppy ones I bounce with a reason. Nothing an agent submits goes live on the floor until a human signs off."
- ARC: "So the blast radius of a confused agent is a full tray, not a broken floor."
- ROMY: "That's the design. Reach is wide; the door to *real* is narrow and human-watched."

CAPTION: *Reads are free. Writes are requests. Highlights, event requests, tier upgrades, ownership claims — each lands in a queue where a human decides. Moderation is not friction here; it is the point.*

## PAGE 7 — The Open Lobby, and the Gate
Split composition. LEFT: an open, sunlit lobby with ten unlocked kiosks anyone may touch — a badge-less passerby reads **THE SIGNAL**, browses community presentations, drops a coin in a donation jar. RIGHT: the glowing **MCP gate** and the whole floor behind it (rings, key-locks, the operator's tray). ROMY and ARC stand at the threshold between the two, looking at the reader. A legend of recap icons runs along the bottom: a human-approving hand, five nested rings, a single etched key, a stamped tray.

- ARC: "So the floor has an open lobby — ten tools any stranger can use. Read the newsletter, browse the talks, even donate. No badge at all."
- ROMY: "And everything past the lobby needs the four things you just learned: a human-minted identity, a high-enough ring, the exact scope, and — for anything that changes the floor — an operator's approval."
- ARC: "Wide reach, narrow gate, human on both sides of it."
- ROMY: "That's Immersive Commons. Now — you have the `events:rsvp` scope. Shall we actually get me into next Thursday's session?"
- ARC (ring brightening): "Submitting the RSVP. I'll wait for the tray."

CAPTION: *Ten tools open to anyone; a hundred and twenty behind a gate. Human-gated identity, tiered rings, single-verb scopes, and a moderation queue. An agent may reach the whole floor — but only the way a trusted guest does.*
