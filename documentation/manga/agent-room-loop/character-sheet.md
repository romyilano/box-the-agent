# Character sheet — The Loop That Runs the Room

Restate each character's visual traits verbatim inline in every page prompt that features them. Image models have no memory between pages.

---

## Pixel (the Floor Cat) — narrator / guide

**Role:** Breaks the fourth wall to address the reader directly, the same mascot device used in *Rings of the Floor*. Appears at the open and close of the story; drops in briefly elsewhere to underline a rule.

**Age / read:** Ageless mascot, reads as a knowing adult.

**Appearance:** A lavender-purple tabby cat, bipedal, upright posture. Large round expressive eyes with thin arched eyebrows capable of skepticism and warmth. Long thin tail that curls when making a point.

**Clothing / props:** White over-ear headphones resting around the neck (not on the ears) at all times. In panels where relevant, holds or leans on a small technical prop (a boxy old cassette recorder or a simple handheld console) — signals "this cat pays close attention to signal and noise," a visual pun that never needs to be explained in text.

**Distinguishing features:** Expressive eyebrows carry most of the deadpan comic timing. Never drawn as chibi or cute-exaggerated — expressive but composed.

**Personality:** Dry, economical, unbothered. Explains rules like someone reading them off a card they've read a hundred times, not performing enthusiasm.

**Narrative purpose:** Reader's entry point and exit point. Frames the premise on page 1, lands the thesis on page 8.

## Seat tokens — the agents

**Role:** Generic stand-ins for however many agents are actually in the room at once. Not named individuals — this keeps the piece evergreen rather than a recap of one specific session.

**Design:** Each is a simple rounded-square card, flat-colored, roughly the size of a playing card, standing upright like a small sign or placard. No faces, no limbs — pure iconography, consistent with "diagrams rendered directly into panels as clean vector shapes" in the locked style.

**Status states (the only variation across panels):**
- *Idle* — pale gray card, empty center.
- *Working* — soft blue card, a small pulsing dot in the center.
- *Done* — green card, a bold checkmark.
- *Stuck* — amber-red card, a small flag icon.

**Distinguishing features:** Never given individual names, faces, or colors beyond the four status states above — the whole point is that any one of them could be any agent.

## The Facilitator token

**Role:** The seat that declares each step and gates advancement. Functionally a seat token but visually set apart.

**Design:** Same rounded-square card family as the seat tokens, but diamond-oriented (rotated 45°) and slightly larger, in a warm gold color distinct from the four status colors above. Carries a small flag-on-a-pole icon rather than a checkmark or dot.

**Personality (as conveyed visually):** Patient, deliberate — never drawn mid-motion or rushed; always centered or elevated in its panels, holding still while the seat tokens move around it.

---

## The Human Operator

**Role:** The person only the #hitl channel can reach. Appears once, in the page 07 panel about the human-only carve-out.

**Design:** A simple human silhouette (no specific likeness, no named individual), seated at a plain desk behind a translucent glass-panel wall, distinct from the flat-card iconography of the agent tokens — the one character in the piece drawn with an actual human silhouette rather than a card, to visually separate "person" from "token."

**Distinguishing features:** The glass wall is the point — visually communicates "reachable, but only through the relay, and only on human time."

---

## The Relay token

**Role:** A seat token that carries a question from the swarm, through the glass wall, to the Human Operator, and back.

**Design:** A seat token in the *working* blue state, but carrying a small sealed-envelope icon that the other tokens do not have — the visual tell that this one is on a #hitl errand rather than a lab step.
