Page 02

Purpose:
Every seated agent attempts the instruction independently and in parallel — no cross-talk during the attempt.

Audience note:
Builds directly on page 01's spotlight moment; assumes the reader just saw all seats turn "working" at once.

Panel Count:
6

Image Prompt:

Create a six-panel educational manga page.

Style:
Clean colored infographic-manga.
Flat soft coloring, warm cream/paper background.
Recurring simple mascot character (expressive, not chibi).
Bold confident linework, generous negative space.
Diagrams (loops, cycles, arrows) rendered directly into panels as clean vector shapes.
Bold sans display lettering for headers; dry, deadpan caption voice.
Not black-and-white. Not gritty. Not glossy 3D. Not photorealistic.

Characters in this page:
Pixel the Floor Cat — a lavender-purple tabby cat, bipedal, upright posture, large round expressive eyes with thin arched eyebrows, long thin tail. White over-ear headphones resting around the neck, not on the ears. Dry, unbothered expression.
Seat tokens — plain rounded-square cards standing upright like small placards, no faces or limbs, flat-colored. Working state: soft blue card with a small pulsing dot in the center.

Setting:
The same open stage from page 01, now redrawn with faint painted lane-divider lines radiating outward from where the ring used to be — as if the circle unfolded into individual racing lanes.

Panel 1:
Wide shot: the ring of tokens from page 01 has unfolded into eight parallel lanes, each with one blue working token at the same starting line, painted lane-divider lines separating them.
Caption: "Working alone is not the same as working apart."

Panel 2:
Close on a single blue token mid-lane, a small gear icon spinning above it to indicate active effort, completely alone in its lane with visible empty space on both sides.
Caption: "Each seat gets the same instruction and its own empty lane."

Panel 3:
Split-panel composition: two adjacent lanes side by side, divided by a bold painted line down the center. The left token is further along than the right token. No arrows or dotted lines cross the divider.
Caption: "No lane reads another lane's work. Not yet."

Panel 4:
Close insert: a small crossed-out speech-balloon icon hovers in the gap between two lanes, a clean vector diagram element rather than a scene object.
Caption: (small in-panel label only) "cross-talk: off"

Panel 5:
Pixel leans casually into frame from outside the lanes, one eyebrow raised, addressing the reader.
Caption: "It looks unfriendly. It's just first."

Panel 6:
Wide shot of all eight lanes again, tokens now at visibly different positions along their lanes — some further ahead, some barely moved — same starting line, clearly diverging progress.
Caption: "Some lanes will finish before others even finish reading the instruction. That's expected. It isn't a problem yet."

Lettering:
Keep the in-panel "cross-talk: off" label in Panel 4 small and diagrammatic, distinct from the caption-box lettering used elsewhere on the page.

Output:
A single finished manga page.
